---
title: Billing and budgets
description: What costs money, how it is paid for, and how to put a limit on it.
---

Two things cost money, and they are separate systems with separate prices:

- **Model use**: the words an AI employee, Nova or a cloud browser sends to and
  gets back from a model. Priced per token, and paid from a prepaid credit
  balance.
- **Machine time**: the machines on [Humanik Cloud](/docs/cloud) that offices,
  cloud browsers and web services run on. Priced per hour, by machine size. See
  [Humanik Cloud pricing](/docs/cloud/pricing).

Knowing which is which is the difference between a spending limit that does what
you expect and one that does not.

Platform access is billed on its own, as a subscription, separately from both.
This section is about the two usage meters that sit on top of it.

## What costs money

| | **Model use** | **Machine time** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it measures | Tokens in and out of a model | Hours a machine runs |
| Priced | Per token, at what the model costs | Per hour, by machine size. See [Pricing](/docs/cloud/pricing) |
| Who does it | Nova, AI employees, cloud browsers | Offices, cloud browsers and web services |
| Paid from | Your prepaid credit, or your own model account | Not drawn from credit |
| Can be limited by a budget | **Yes** | **No** |

**An AI employee uses both.** Its office is billed for the hours it is awake, and
each piece of model work it does is charged to credit on top of that.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Only one of the two can be limited.** A budget covers model use. It does not
  cover the cost of an office being awake, and there is no equivalent limit for
  that today. An office left running keeps costing until it sleeps.
</Callout>

The practical consequence: a budget protects you from model work that keeps
spending, not from a machine nobody turned off. For that, give offices an idle timeout so
they sleep on their own. See [Machine time](#machine-time) below.

**Nothing adds the two together.** They are measured by different systems and
reported separately, so "what did this cost me in total" is a question you
answer by looking in two places.

## Who pays for model use

Every piece of model work is paid one of two ways, decided by which model
account it runs on.

| | **Our model account** | **Your own model account** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who pays the provider | We do, and charge your credit | You do, directly |
| Draws on your credit | Yes | **No. We never bill it** |
| Appears in usage records | Yes, with what was charged | Yes, with what it cost you, and nothing charged |
| Can be limited by a budget | Yes | **Yes** |

The second column is the one most people do not expect. When an employee runs
on [your own model account](/docs/models/your-own-key), the provider bills you
and we charge nothing. We still record what the work cost, so you can see it,
and a budget can still limit it.

**The default model choice spends credit.** Leaving the model set to automatic
is not a free option. It picks a model for you and charges it like any other,
even when your workspace has your own account available. See
[Models](/docs/models).

## The checks before model work runs

Work by Nova and by AI employees passes the same checks, in order, before it
starts and again while it runs:

```
        which account pays?
                │
      ┌─────────┴─────────┐
      │                   │
   your own             ours
      │                   │
      │            enough credit? ──── no ──▶ stops
      │                   │
      │                  yes
      └─────────┬─────────┘
                │
     a budget that stops work
     at its limit, and counts
       this kind of spend? ──── yes ──▶ stops
                │
                no
                │
              runs
```

Read the shape rather than the boxes. **Your own model account skips the credit
check**, because there is nothing of ours to draw down. It does not skip
budgets. That is why a budget is the only control that covers both.

[Credit and top ups](/docs/spend/credit) covers the credit check, and
[Budgets](/docs/spend/budgets) covers the last one, including budgets that warn
or only send alerts instead of stopping work.

## When work stops for a money reason

Nova answers with a `402` and a `code` saying which check failed.

| `code` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `insufficient_balance` | Out of credit. Add credit, switch on automatic top ups, or use your own model account |
| `budget_exceeded` | A budget reached its limit. Nova also returns which budget, its window and scope, the amount used and the limit |

Branch on `code` rather than the message text. Nova returns exactly these from
[Send a prompt](/docs/api-reference/nova/running-nova/runNovaCycle).

**When it arrives depends on the check.** Running out of credit can return a
`402` at the start of a turn or partway through one. A budget returns a `402`
only when it stops a turn before it starts. A budget reached partway through
ends the turn with a `200` and a `status` of `budget_exceeded`, so check the
body as well as the status code.

## Who can see and change billing

Billing is an account concern. Everything about credit, usage, invoices and
compute is checked at `console`.

| To | You need |
| --- | --- |
| See the balance, usage, invoices, compute cost and every budget | `billing:read` @ `console` |
| Configure automatic top ups, add or remove cards, buy credit, manage any budget | `billing:manage` @ `console` |
| See the budgets on one workspace and its employees | `workspace:read` @ that workspace |
| Create and change budgets on one workspace and its employees | `workspace:manage` @ that workspace |

The built-in [`consoleAdmin`](/docs/access-control/roles) role holds both billing
permissions, and the account owner is never refused. `workspaceAdmin` holds
`workspace:manage`, which is what lets you give a team lead control of their own
team's limits without showing them the account's finances.

**Paying is a console flow.** Buying credit, saving cards, choosing the default
card and paying subscription invoices are done in the console and refuse API
keys. Reading the balance, usage and invoices, configuring automatic top ups,
and managing budgets all work with a key. See the
[Billing API](/docs/api-reference/billing).

## Machine time

Every price, the exact rules for which time is billed, and worked examples are on
[Humanik Cloud pricing](/docs/cloud/pricing). In short: each machine is billed
for the time it runs at the hourly price of its size, and a machine that is
asleep costs nothing to run.

[Get compute spend](/docs/api-reference/billing/compute/getComputeSpendSummary)
breaks machine time down by service, and matches each machine to its office, so
you can see which employee it belongs to. Anything running that is not an office
is listed separately.

Two settings decide what a service costs, and they are the only levers you have:

- **Machine size.** A smaller size has a lower hourly price. See
  [Machine sizes](/docs/cloud/sizes).
- **Idle timeout.** A shorter timeout means fewer hours awake. See
  [Scaling and sleep](/docs/cloud/scaling).

Change them with
[Change a service's settings](/docs/api-reference/billing/compute/updateComputeServiceConfig).
