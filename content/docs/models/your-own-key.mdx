---
title: Bring your own key
description: Running on your own vendor account instead of platform credit.
---

If you already have an account with a model vendor, your AI employees can use it.
Work is billed to you by them, at your rates, and nothing goes through your
platform credit.

## Why bother

**Your rates and your invoice.** You are billed by the vendor directly, on
whatever terms you have with them. Volume discounts and committed-spend
agreements apply, which they cannot when the account is ours.

**Far more models.** An OpenRouter account opens 300 or so models across
essentially every vendor, and the list is live rather than a set we curate.

**Claude at all.** Claude models cannot be bought with platform credit. Your own
Anthropic account is the only way to reach them.

**As narrow as you like.** The choice goes down to a single office, and for Nova
down to a single person. It is not an account-wide switch.

## Two kinds of account

| | Gives you |
| --- | --- |
| **Anthropic** | Claude models, billed to your Anthropic account |
| **OpenRouter** | 300+ models across most vendors, billed to your OpenRouter account |

One of each per account. Storing a second key for the same vendor replaces the
first, and there is no history and no rotation window, so treat it as a
replacement rather than a rotation.

## Storing the key

**This part is the console, and only an owner can do it.** There is no API for
it, deliberately: holding the credential is an owner-only act, while *using* it
is delegated.

In the console, go to the account's model provider settings, choose the vendor,
and paste the key. What gets stored is the key itself in a vault, plus its last
four characters so you can tell later which key is in place without revealing it.

<Callout>
  **An Anthropic key is checked before it is stored.** An OpenRouter key is not,
  and verifies the first time something uses it, so a typo shows up as a failed
  message rather than a failed save.
</Callout>

## Giving it to a workspace

A stored key is used by nothing. It has to be given to each workspace that should
use it, which is also a console step.

This is what makes the model list honest: a workspace sees "your key" models
**only** when it genuinely holds that key.

<Callout type="warn">
  **The console has its own workspace.** Nova in the dashboard, outside any
  workspace, needs its own assignment. Storing a key for the account does not opt
  it in.
</Callout>

Before removing a workspace's access, the console can show you which offices that
would revert to platform billing. Worth looking at, because those offices do not
change until they restart.

## Opting an office in

Once a workspace holds a key, each office decides separately whether to use it.
That decision is API-reachable, unlike the two steps above:

```bash
curl -X PUT "$HOS_API/cloud-claw/byok-config?workspaceId=ws_a1b2c3&officeId=off_dispatch" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "useCustomKey": true,
    "provider": "anthropic",
    "modelId": "claude-sonnet-4-5",
    "fallbackModelId": "openai/gpt-4o-mini"
  }'
```

See
[Set an office's model provider](/docs/api-reference/offices/model-provider/updateOfficeModelProvider).

Three things about that call are worth knowing.

**It applies at the next restart.** An office decides once, when it starts. A
running office keeps what it booted with, so apply the change with
[Restart an office](/docs/api-reference/offices/machine/restartOfficeMachine). That replaces the machine, which is what makes the new setting land.

**Send `provider` when the workspace holds more than one.** Leave it out and the
office keeps its current choice, or takes the only assignment when there is
exactly one. With two assigned, it will not guess.

**Always set a fallback.** `fallbackModelId` is a platform-billed model that does
two jobs: it is what the office runs when it is not using your account, and what
it falls back to if your account cannot be reached. Set it before you turn
anything else on.

## And then choose a model

<Callout type="warn">
  **Nothing above changes what Nova runs on.** Each person still has to pick a
  model, because the default is Auto and Auto uses our account. See
  [Choosing a model](/docs/models/choosing-a-model).
</Callout>

For offices the model is part of the same call, so opting in and choosing happen
together. For Nova they are separate, and the second half is the one people miss.

## Who can do what

Three tiers, deliberately different:

| Action | Needed | Who typically has it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store a key, give it to a workspace | `tenant:manage` @ console | The owner only |
| Point an office at it | `office:manage` @ that office | Workspace admin, office operator |
| See the model list, read a preference | `nova:read` @ workspace | Everyone with access |
| Change your own model | `nova:manage` @ workspace | Workspace admin |

An office operator can point their own office at the account's key **without
being able to see, store, or reveal it.** That split is the point: holding the
credential and using it are different privileges.

## What this does not do

**It does not cap spend.** Your vendor bills you directly. A
[spending limit](/docs/spend) can still cap what employees spend on your own
account, which is the only control that reaches money we never touch.

**There is no per-key usage report.** We record who assigned what and when.
Attributing spend to a particular key is your vendor's dashboard, not ours.

**It does not rotate.** Storing a key replaces the previous one immediately.
