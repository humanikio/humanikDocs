Every AI employee and every Nova conversation runs on a model. By default that
model is ours, and the cost comes out of your credit.

You can change that in two different ways, and they are not variations of one
idea:

| | What you bring | What it gets you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[Your own key](/docs/models/your-own-key)** | An account with a model vendor | Your rates, your invoice, your choice of 300+ models |
| **[Your own compute](/docs/models/your-own-compute)** | A machine you own | No vendor and no per-token cost, and nothing leaves your building |

The first is about billing. The second is about where the work physically
happens. Most of this section applies to both, so start here.

## The four layers

Setting this up is four steps, and **each one is independent of the others**.

<Steps>

<Step>
**Store the credential**, once for the account. Done in the console, and only an
owner can do it.
</Step>

<Step>
**Give it to a workspace.** A stored credential is used by nothing until a
workspace holds it.
</Step>

<Step>
**Opt an office in**, if you want that employee using it rather than credits.
</Step>

<Step>
**Choose a model.** This is the step that actually changes what runs.
</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="warn">
  **Steps 1 to 3 change nothing on their own.** It is entirely possible to store
  a key, assign every workspace, opt in every office, and still be billed for
  every message, because nobody performed step 4. It is the step most often
  skipped, and the only one that has an effect you can see on an invoice.
</Callout>

## The default runs on credits, deliberately

Nova's model picker defaults to **Auto**, and Auto always uses our key and always
draws your credit, even when your workspace has your own account assigned.

That is on purpose rather than an oversight.

> **Pick a model to start using your own account.** Auto means we choose, so we
> pay. We will not spend your account on your behalf until you name a model
> yourself.

Naming a model is the act of consent. It is also the thing to check first when
your own account looks like it is not being used: almost always, the people using
that workspace never left Auto.

## What decides who pays

The model identifier carries the answer. You do not set billing separately, and
there is no switch for it: choosing a model is choosing who pays.

| What you pick | Runs on | Billed to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A model under **Local machine** | That exact machine | Nobody. There is no per-token cost |
| A model under **Your key** | Your vendor account | Your vendor invoice |
| A model under **Platform models** | Our account | Your credit |
| **Auto** | Our account | Your credit |

The same model can appear in two sections at once, which looks redundant and is
not: one row spends your account and the other spends your credit. That is why
the section a model sits under matters more than its name.

## What each workspace can see

The picker shows a workspace **only the providers that workspace has been given**.
This is a deliberate consequence of layer 2: "Your key" in the list always means
*this workspace genuinely uses that account*, never *the account exists
somewhere*.

One consequence worth knowing before you go looking for a model that is missing:

<Callout type="warn">
  **Claude models require your own Anthropic account.** They cannot be bought
  with platform credit. A workspace without an Anthropic assignment does not see
  them greyed out, it does not see them at all.
</Callout>

## Nova and offices behave oppositely

The same setup lands at different times depending on who is thinking.

| | Nova | An office |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decides which model on | Every message | Once, when it starts |
| Chosen by | Each person, for themselves | The office's own configuration |
| A change applies | Next message | **Next restart** |

So removing a workspace's access stops Nova immediately and does **not** stop an
office that is already running. An office keeps whatever it booted with until it
restarts. Applying a change means [Restart an office](/docs/api-reference/offices/machine/restartOfficeMachine), which replaces the machine rather
than rebooting it.

This one distinction causes more confusion than anything else here, and it is
worth holding onto: **Nova asks every time, an office asks once.**
