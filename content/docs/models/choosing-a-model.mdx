---
title: Choosing a model
description: Per person, per office, and when a smaller model is the better answer.
---

Two separate choices, made by different people at different times.

| | Who chooses | When it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nova** | Each person, for themselves | The next message |
| **An office** | Whoever configures the office | The next restart, see below |

Nova's choice is personal: two people in one workspace can be on different
models, and neither affects the other. An office's choice is the office's, and
everyone it works for gets it.

## Choosing for yourself in Nova

Read what your workspace can reach:

```bash
curl "$HOS_API/nova/models?workspaceId=ws_a1b2c3" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOS_API_KEY"
```

Then set yours:

```bash
curl -X PATCH "$HOS_API/nova/model-preference?workspaceId=ws_a1b2c3" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{ "modelId": "claude-sonnet-4-5" }'
```

See [List models](/docs/api-reference/nova/preferences/listNovaModels) and
[Set model preference](/docs/api-reference/nova/preferences/upsertNovaModelPreference).

<Callout type="warn">
  **The value is not checked against the list when you save it.** A model that
  does not resolve is stored happily and shows up as a failed message later. Take
  it from the list rather than typing it.
</Callout>

## Choosing for an office

An office carries two model settings, and both matter:

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `modelId` | What the office runs on |
| `fallbackModelId` | A platform-billed model, used when the office is not on your own account, and when that account cannot be reached |

**Set the fallback even if you never expect to need it.** It is not only a
safety net: it is what the office actually runs whenever it is not using your own
account, so an office without one has no defined answer to a question it will be
asked at every boot.

```bash
curl -X PUT "$HOS_API/cloud-claw/byok-config?workspaceId=ws_a1b2c3&officeId=off_dispatch" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "useCustomKey": true,
    "provider": "openrouter",
    "modelId": "anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5",
    "fallbackModelId": "openai/gpt-4o-mini"
  }'
```

### What an office falls back to

An office works down a list at boot, taking the first thing it finds:

<Steps>

<Step>
**The model set on this office.** The specific choice, if there is one.
</Step>

<Step>
**Whatever its workspace was configured with**, for offices set up before per-office
choice existed.
</Step>

<Step>
**The provider's own default**, if nothing above answered.
</Step>

</Steps>

Whatever it lands on, a change only takes hold when the office next starts, and
starting it properly means [Restart an office](/docs/api-reference/offices/machine/restartOfficeMachine).

So an office with no model does not fail to start. It starts on something, and
the something may not be what you assumed. Read it back with
[Get an office's model provider](/docs/api-reference/offices/model-provider/getOfficeModelProvider)
rather than inferring it.

## Who can change what

The two choices are governed differently, which matches who they affect.

| Action | Needed |
| --- | --- |
| See what models a workspace can reach | `nova:read` @ workspace |
| Change your own model in Nova | `nova:manage` @ workspace |
| Change an office's model or machine | `office:manage` @ that office |

Your Nova choice is yours alone, so it needs no authority over anyone else. An
office's choice affects everything that office does for everybody, which is why
it sits with whoever administers the office.

Neither lets you add a provider. Bringing a vendor account or a machine into a
workspace is owner-only and console-only. See
[Bring your own key](/docs/models/your-own-key#who-can-do-what).

## When a smaller model is the right answer

The strongest model is not the right default for everything, and on a busy office
the difference is not small.

**A smaller model is usually right when the work is shaped rather than open
ended.** Extracting fields from a document, classifying a message, deciding which
of four branches to take, summarising something short, tidying formatting. The
answer is constrained by the task, so extra reasoning buys little.

**Reach for the strongest model when the work is genuinely open.** Multi-step
plans where one wrong turn wastes the rest, writing that has to be good rather
than correct, reading code, anything where the employee has to decide what the
question even is.

A useful test: **if you could describe the answer's shape before seeing it, a
smaller model will probably produce it.** If you could not, pay for the reasoning.

**A workflow is the third option and often the best one.** If a step is fully
determined, a [workflow](/docs/workflows) does it identically every time for no
model cost at all. Reserve the model for the step that actually requires
judgement.

### Mixing them deliberately

Nothing requires one model everywhere. A common arrangement:

- The office that handles inbound triage runs on something small and quick
- The office that drafts customer-facing replies runs on the strongest you have
- People running Nova pick per person, because their work varies more than an
  office's does

Since an office's choice is per office, this costs nothing to arrange beyond
deciding it.
