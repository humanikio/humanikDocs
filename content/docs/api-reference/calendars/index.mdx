A calendar here is a container you create and bind provider accounts to. Reads
and writes pass through to those providers, so nothing is duplicated and nothing
drifts.

## Getting one working

A new calendar shows nothing and nobody can use it. Two more steps fix that.

<Steps>

<Step>
**Create it.**
[Create a calendar](/docs/api-reference/calendars/calendars/createCalendar)
takes a name and a workspace.
</Step>

<Step>
**Bind a source.**
[List connectable calendar accounts](/docs/api-reference/calendars/sources/listAvailableSources)
gives you an `accountId`, then
[Bind a source](/docs/api-reference/calendars/sources/bindCalendarSource) points
the calendar at it. Bind several and they merge into one view.
</Step>

<Step>
**Add members.**
[Add a member](/docs/api-reference/calendars/members/addCalendarMember) for each
person or AI employee who needs it. Until this, even you cannot list events.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Membership is the thing that authorises

This is the part that surprises people, and it is worth getting right before you
debug a `403`.

A calendar belongs to a workspace, but the workspace is **not** what decides who
may use it. The member list is.

| | Needs membership |
| --- | --- |
| Reading and writing events and tasks | **Yes.** Holding `calendar:manage` is not enough |
| Reading the calendar, members, sources | Membership **or** `calendar:manage` |
| Governing it: rename, bind, delete, members | No. The role alone is enough |
| Booking links | No. Permission alone, with no membership layer |

A member from **any** workspace passes. So sharing a calendar across teams is
adding people to its member list, not moving anything.

<Callout type="warn">
  **A `403` here has two causes.** Either the role is missing, or the role is
  there and the caller is not a member. Both look identical. Check
  [List members](/docs/api-reference/calendars/members/listCalendarMembers)
  before assuming the grant is wrong.
</Callout>

## An AI employee is a member like anyone else

An office joins the member list as an ordinary row, with `memberType: "office"`,
and then calls the same endpoints a person calls through the same checks. There
is no separate agent path and no elevated access.

This is the cleanest example of the idea on the platform: give an employee a
calendar the same way you give a colleague one, and remove it the same way.

```bash
curl -X POST "$HOS_API/calendars/cal_7Kd2mQ/members" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $HOS_API_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "memberId": "off_dispatch",
    "memberType": "office",
    "workspaceId": "ws_a1b2c3",
    "displayName": "Dispatch"
  }'
```

## Writes go to a provider, not to us

Creating an event writes it into the Google or Outlook account you name. Two
consequences worth planning for:

- **You must say where.** Every event and task write takes an `accountId` and the
  identifier of the calendar or list inside it.
- **Deleting a calendar deletes nothing at the provider.** You lose the
  container, its members and its bindings. The events stay where they were
  written.

## Booking links

A booking link is a page someone outside your account opens to pick a slot.
Bookings land on this calendar and can start an automation.

### Blocking time out

Availability is computed from five things, and understanding which one to reach
for saves a lot of guessing.

| To block | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A recurring day or part of a day | `workingHours`. A weekday you omit has no availability at all |
| One specific date | `dateOverrides`, with `[]` for that date |
| Around real commitments | Nothing. Events on the bound calendars already remove slots automatically |
| Padding around each booking | `bufferBeforeMin` and `bufferAfterMin` |
| Volume | `maxPerDay` and `maxPerSlot` |

A date in `dateOverrides` **replaces** that day's working hours rather than
adding to them, which is what makes `[]` mean closed.

Existing events are the part you do not configure. Anything on a bound calendar
that is not marked free removes the slots it overlaps, so blocking an afternoon
is a matter of putting something in your own calendar.

<Callout type="warn">
  **A day with `maxPerDay` set cannot be rescheduled within itself.** A booking
  being moved still counts toward the day it is leaving, so once the cap is
  reached, moving it to a later slot the same day is refused. Moving it to
  another day works.
</Callout>

Two more behaviours to know:

<Callout type="warn">
  **Slugs are unique across the whole platform.** A slug already taken by
  somebody else is rejected even though it is not taken by you. Prefer something
  specific.
</Callout>

<Callout>
  **Deleting a link with bookings ahead of it returns `409`, not `404`.** That is
  a refusal, not a failure: deleting would strand people who already booked. Send
  `?force=true` to override, or disable the link instead to stop new bookings
  while keeping the ones you have.
</Callout>
