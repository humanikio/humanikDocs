---
title: Spending
description: The two meters, and how to put a ceiling on them.
---

An AI employee costs money on two axes, and the platform treats them as separate
systems. Knowing which is which is the difference between a spending limit that
does what you expect and one that quietly does not.

Platform access is billed on its own, separately from both. This page is about
the two usage meters that sit on top of it.

## The two meters

| | **Model use** | **Compute** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it measures | Words in and out of a model | An office being awake |
| Charged against | Your prepaid credit | Machine time, billed separately |
| Can be capped | **Yes**, by a spending limit | **No** |

<Callout type="warn">
  **Only one of the two can be capped.** A spending limit covers model use. It
  does not cover the cost of an office being awake, and there is no equivalent
  ceiling for that today. An office left running accrues cost that no limit on
  this page will stop.
</Callout>

The practical consequence: a spending limit protects you from a runaway
conversation, not from a machine nobody turned off. For that, give offices an
idle timeout so they sleep on their own.

## Credit, and what draws it down

Model use is paid from a prepaid balance. Work deducts from it, and when it runs
low the platform can charge your saved card and top it back up automatically.

Two behaviours worth knowing.

**A failed top-up does not interrupt work already running.** It surfaces on the
*next* piece of work, as an out-of-credit error. So a declined card shows up
later than you would expect, and not where it failed.

**The default model choice spends credit.** Leaving the model set to automatic
is not a free option. It picks a model for you and charges it like any other, and
it does so even when your workspace has your own account available. See
[Models](/docs/models).

## Spending limits

A limit is a ceiling on model spend in **one workspace**, over a day or a month.
Past the ceiling, work in that workspace stops until the window resets.

Set one with
[Create a budget](/docs/api-reference/workspaces/budgets/createWorkspaceBudget).

### It can cap your own model account too

This is the part most people miss, and it is the reason limits are the right
tool rather than credit.

If you [bring your own model account](/docs/models/your-own-key), that usage
never touches your credit here and we never bill you for it. **A limit can still
cap it.** You choose whether it
counts model use we bill, model use we do not, or both.

So the answer to *"how do I stop my AI employees running up my model bill?"* is a
spending limit, and it works whether or not we are the ones being paid. A credit
balance cannot do that, because there is no balance to draw down.

### Three checks before anything spends

Every piece of work that would use a model passes the same three checks, in
order:

```
        which account pays?
                │
      ┌─────────┴─────────┐
      │                   │
   your own            ours
      │                   │
      │            enough credit? ──── no ──▶ stops
      │                   │
      │                  yes
      └─────────┬─────────┘
                │
        under the limit? ──── no ──▶ stops
                │
               yes
                │
              runs
```

Read the shape rather than the boxes. **Your own model account skips the credit
check**, because there is nothing of ours to draw down. It does not skip the
spending limit. That is why a limit is the only control that covers both.

### What a limit will not do

<Callout type="warn">
  **It stops work rather than warning you.** There is no notify-only limit and no
  alert before the ceiling. When you reach it, work stops.
</Callout>

Two more, worth setting expectations on:

- **It is not a total spend cap.** Machine time is not counted, as above.
- **There is no combined figure.** The two meters are reported separately and
  nothing adds them into one number, so "what did this cost me" is a question you
  answer by looking in two places.

### Windows follow the workspace

A daily limit resets on the workspace's own calendar day, using
[its timezone](/docs/api-reference/workspaces/workspaces/updateWorkspaceTimeConfig).
Two workspaces in different regions roll over at different moments, which is
what you want when they serve customers in different places.

## When work stops for a spending reason

You get a `402`, with a code saying which check failed.

| `code` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `insufficient_balance` | Out of credit. Top up, or use your own model account |
| `budget_exceeded` | A limit was reached. Comes with the window, the amount used and the ceiling |

Branch on `code` rather than the message text. Nova returns exactly these from
[Send a prompt](/docs/api-reference/nova/running-nova/runNovaCycle), and the
same pair appears anywhere else model work is refused.

An out-of-credit error can arrive at the start of a piece of work or partway
through one, since the check runs again as work continues.
