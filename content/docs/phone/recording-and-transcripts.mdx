---
title: Recording and transcripts
description: What is kept from a call, who can play it back, and where the words come from.
---

Two different things come out of a call, and they are produced two different
ways. A **transcript** is text of what was said. A **recording** is the audio.
You can have either without the other.

## Recording is off until you turn it on

There is no recording switch at the phone company and nothing to reconcile
upstream. The decision is made per call, at the moment the call starts, by
reading the number's settings. Turning recording off stops the next call from
being recorded, with nothing left over.

Two settings, both off by default:

| Setting | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| `recordOutboundOffice` | Calls your employee is on |
| `recordOutboundStaff` | Calls your people place from the browser |

<Callout type="warn">
  **The first one is named for outbound calls and covers inbound calls too.** An
  employee's calls are recorded in both directions when it is on. If you enable
  it to capture calls the employee places, you are also recording the calls it
  answers.
</Callout>

Recordings are dual channel, one side of the conversation per channel, so the two
speakers stay separable.

### Playing one back

A recording is not a public link. Playing one goes through us, needs `phone:read`
in the workspace that owns the call, and is checked against where the call
actually happened rather than against the workspace the request claims.

That means a recording cannot be played by guessing an identifier, and a link
copied out of the console does not work for someone without permission.

## Where the words come from

**A call your employee holds is transcribed as part of the conversation.** The
caller's speech is turned into text so the employee can answer it, and that text
is the transcript. It is written turn by turn, as it happens, so a call that ends
badly still leaves everything said up to that point.

**A call between two people is transcribed only if it is recorded.** Transcription
is deliberately coupled to recording on those calls: if you have decided not to
keep the audio, we do not keep a written copy of it either. Turning recording on
turns both on, with each side labelled.

**Voicemail is not transcribed at all.** A message is audio, and someone has to
listen to it.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Transcription is English today.** Speech in another language is not
  transcribed. On an employee call that also means the employee does not hear it,
  because the text is what reaches it.
</Callout>

## Everything lands in one place

Whatever is produced goes onto the call's record in the contact's conversation,
next to their texts and their email. There is no call log to reconcile with the
CRM, because there is no separate call log.

A recording arrives later than the call does. The audio is finalised after
everyone hangs up, so a call appears in the conversation immediately and its
recording attaches to the same record when it is ready.

## Before you turn recording on

Recording and transcribing a call is regulated, and the rules depend on where
both parties are. Some places require both sides to consent, some require one,
and some require an announcement at the start of the call.

Nothing here announces anything. The employee's greeting is the place to say it,
if saying it is what you need. See [Who answers](/docs/phone/answering).
