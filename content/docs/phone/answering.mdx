title: Who answers
description: Sending a number to your employee, to your people in order, or to both.
---

Every number decides for itself who picks up. One line can go straight to an
employee while another rings three people first and falls back to the employee
behind them.

<AnswerModes />

The default is `ai`. A number you have just allocated is answered by the employee
holding it, with voicemail behind it.

## Ringing your people

Both staff modes work the same way: an ordered list of people, rung one at a
time, in the order you set.

<Steps>

<Step>
**Each person adds their own number.** A call forwarding number is set by the
person themselves, in their workspace settings. Nobody adds it on their behalf.
</Step>

<Step>
**An administrator builds the ring order.** The picker shows who has a number
configured and lets you order them. It never shows the number.
</Step>

<Step>
**The cascade runs in order.** Each person is rung for a set time before the call
moves to the next one. The default is fifteen seconds per person.
</Step>

<Step>
**Whoever answers presses 1 to take it.** Then the caller is connected. This step
is on by default and can be turned off.
</Step>

<Step>
**If nobody takes it**, `staff-first` hands the call to the employee, and
`staff-only` goes to voicemail.
</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout>
  **A staff member's personal number is never shown to anyone.** The ring order
  picker returns whether a person has a number configured and nothing more, so an
  administrator can put someone in the order without ever seeing what they are
  dialing.
</Callout>

### Why the keypress is there

Without it, a personal voicemail can hijack the call. A phone that is off, or
declined, sends the call to that person's own voicemail, which answers, which
looks to the cascade exactly like a human picking up. The caller would then be
recording a message on somebody's personal phone instead of moving to the next
person on the list.

Requiring a keypress makes the difference visible. A voicemail box does not press
1, so an unconfirmed leg counts as no answer and the cascade continues.

Turn it off only where every number in the ring order is a phone that a person
actually answers.

## What the employee says first

When an employee answers, it opens the call. The default is a short line asking
how it can help.

You can replace it with your own, per number, and an empty greeting is a real
setting: the employee answers and says nothing, leaving the caller to speak
first.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Silence is not a good default, which is why it is not the default.** A call
  answered into silence usually means the caller talks first, into what feels to
  them like a dead line. Set an empty greeting deliberately, not to save a
  second.
</Callout>

## When the employee cannot take the call

An employee that has been idle is not running, and starting one takes longer than
a caller will wait. When a call arrives for a sleeping employee the caller rings
for about eight seconds and reaches voicemail, while the machine wakes in the
background. The next call connects normally.

This applies to `ai` and to `staff-first` after the cascade has run out. See
[The path of a call](/docs/phone/how-a-call-works) for why it works this way, and
[Where offices run](/docs/cloud) for keeping an employee awake.

## Configuring it

Answer mode, ring order, timeout, the keypress and the greeting are all part of
one voice configuration, set per number, in the console under **Routing**. The
settings and their defaults are listed in
[Configuring a number](/docs/phone/config#answering-a-call).

<Callout type="warn">
  **A new number answers nothing until you choose.** The default is
  `voicemail-only`. Handing a number to an employee does not by itself put that
  employee on the phone: you may have done that so it can send texts. Turning
  answering on is a separate choice, made on the number's Routing tab.
</Callout>
