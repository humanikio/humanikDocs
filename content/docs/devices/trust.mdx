---
title: One catalog, two environments
description: Where an agent's tools run across your machine, the cloud and its office, and what never leaves each place.
---

HumanikOS runs an employee in **two environments at once**: the machine you enrol, and the
**office sandbox** HumanikOS runs for that employee. The point of the system is that you do not
have to choose between them. The same employee does local work on your hardware and credentialed
work in its office, inside a single turn, and you never wire the two together by hand.

This page is the whole picture in one place: the three ways an agent acts, where each one runs,
and the three things that stay put no matter which one it is. The pages it links to explain each
mechanism in detail.

## Three ways an agent acts

An agent never experiences the two environments as separate. Everything is a tool with a name.
But *where* a tool runs decides what it can touch and what crosses the connection between your
machine and HumanikOS.

<WhereToolsRun />

Those three modes are reached through **one tool catalog**, defined once in the office sandbox.
The office's own agent uses the catalog inside the sandbox. When the turn runs on your machine
instead, the same catalog is bridged to the agent there. One list, one definition, whichever
machine does the work.

## Where each tool runs

| The agent is… | Runs where | Bounded by | Credentials it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Using a tool on your machine | Your machine, in a workspace directory the daemon derives | **Your ceiling**, in a file you write | Only the connectors you set up locally |
| Running a job sent from the cloud | Your machine, within that same ceiling | **Your ceiling** | Same as above |
| Using an office tool over the bridge | The **office sandbox**, never your hardware | The **employee's permissions**, in the office | The office's own credentials — never copied to you |

The first two stay on your machine and answer to the ceiling you set. The third runs in the
sandbox under the office's permissions. Your machine receives the result, not the credential and
not the code.

## Three things that stay put

**Your keys stay in the office.** The credentials your plugins use, such as your Stripe key,
your CRM token or an internal API credential, are written into a vault the office reads. They
become tools the employee calls, but the values are never sent to your machine, never written
there, and not readable from it. This machine receives responses, not keys. See
[Plugins and secrets](/docs/plugins).

**Your ceiling bounds your machine, and nothing widens it.** The ceiling lives in a config file
on your hardware, written by you. HumanikOS never sends a tool list, a working directory or a
permission mode — it sends the conversation and what it wants done. If the control plane were
compromised tomorrow, it still could not widen what any enrolled machine is permitted to do. See
[the rule that makes this safe](/docs/devices#the-rule-that-makes-this-safe).

**Each hop proves who it is.** The connection your machine opens is outbound only; there is no
inbound port. Your machine signs what it sends with a key that never leaves the device, and the
office a tool call belongs to is taken from the job on our side, never from anything the machine
claims about itself. The identity of a request is established server-side and cannot be forged by
a compromised machine.

<Callout>
  **One catalog is the reason a device is not a lesser employee.** An agent on your laptop
  reaches the same software as one in the cloud, because both read the same catalog. Build a
  plugin once, enable its endpoints, and every agent, in the office or on an enrolled
  machine, can call them, with the credential staying in the office.
</Callout>

## The three boundaries, held by three parties

The guarantees above are not one setting. Work reaching an enrolled machine passes three separate
boundaries, held by three different people, and they compose without leaking into each other.

| Boundary | Held by | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The ceiling** | You, on your machine | What may be done **to your machine** |
| **The allocation** | An administrator, in the console | Which workspace may use the machine, and which employee is attached to it |
| **The employee's permissions** | The permission system | What the employee may reach **in its office** |

Widening the ceiling does not grant the employee more access to company data. Granting the
employee a broader role does not let it do more on your laptop. An administrator pointing an
office at a machine grants the right to think there, not to act beyond the ceiling. The full
treatment is [Three limits, and none can widen another](/docs/devices#three-limits-and-none-can-widen-another).
