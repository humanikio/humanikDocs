---
title: Roles
description: The built-in roles, what each is for, and how to vary one safely.
---

A role is a named bundle of policies. It says what is permitted **in principle**.
Where those permissions apply is decided by the
[assignment](/docs/access-control/assignments), not by the role.

Built-in roles ship with the platform and resolve before custom ones.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Built-in roles and policies cannot be modified or deleted.** This is what
  makes them safe to reason about: a role name means the same thing in every
  tenant. To vary one, fork it into a custom role.
</Callout>

## Built-in roles

Grouped by what they are for.

### Organisation administration

Assigned at `console`. These do not descend into workspaces.

| Role | For |
| --- | --- |
| `consoleAdmin` | Full organisation level administration |
| `membersManager` | Inviting and managing people, without wider console rights |
| `iamManager` | Defining roles and policies, and making assignments |

### Workspace and membership

| Role | For |
| --- | --- |
| `workspaceAdmin` | Full control of one workspace and everything inside it |
| `tenantMember` | Baseline membership. The floor a person needs to use the product. |
| `viewer` | Read only |

### Operational

Narrower roles for a specific job, meant to be assigned at a narrow boundary.

| Role | For |
| --- | --- |
| `officeOperator` | Running and managing offices |
| `workflowOperator` | Running workflows |
| `dataSourceManager` | Managing data sources and ingestion |
| `integrationPublisher` | Publishing integrations for others to use |

### Machine identities

Assigned to service accounts rather than to people. Usually attached
automatically when the office or browser is created.

| Role | For |
| --- | --- |
| `officeAgent` | The identity an AI employee's office runs as |
| `browserAgent` | The identity a cloud browser runs as |

`officeAgent` covers what an AI employee does on your behalf: group chats,
contacts and opportunities, calendars, outbound messages and text messages from
its own assigned addresses and numbers, **placing and ending phone calls**, the
data plane, and the app containers it builds and runs, including their
environment variables. It is scoped to a single workspace.

Phone calls are worth calling out, because it is the permission with the largest
reach outside your workspace. An employee can place a call only from a number
assigned to its office, and only while that office has one. If you do not want an
employee dialling out, do not assign it a number.

It deliberately stops short of destructive and administrative actions. It cannot
delete a project or container, manage workspace settings, add or remove chat
members, or send from an address it was not assigned. Those stay with people.

`browserAgent` is a good illustration of the model working. It is scoped to
inference and the databases allocated to that browser, and it explicitly excludes
chat, contacts, calendars, and office access. A browser that is compromised
cannot reach the rest of the workspace, because its role never permitted it.

## Choosing one

Two questions, in this order:

1. **What is the narrowest role that covers the job?** Start from the operational
   roles. Reach for `workspaceAdmin` only when the job really is "everything in
   this workspace".
2. **What is the narrowest boundary it can be assigned at?** This matters more
   than the choice of role. `viewer` at `workspace/W1` is far wider than
   `officeOperator` at one office.

A common mistake is picking a narrow role and then assigning it at the workspace
root, which quietly grants it everywhere inside.

## Forking a built-in

When a built-in is close but not right, copy it into a custom role rather than
trying to work around it.

In the console, go to **Organization → IAM → Roles**, open the built-in you want
to start from, and create a new role carrying the policies you want. Custom roles
live in your tenant and resolve after built-ins.

<Callout>
  Prefer forking over stacking. Assigning two roles to cover a gap works, but the
  result is harder to audit later, because no single object states what the
  identity may do.
</Callout>

## Policies inside a role

A policy is a list of statements. Each statement has an effect, a set of actions,
and a set of resources:

```json
{
  "effect": "allow",
  "actions": ["office:read", "office:manage"],
  "resources": ["workspace/W1/office/*"]
}
```

Deny statements are evaluated first and win outright. Use them to carve an
exception out of a broad allow, and remember that they do not constrain the
tenant owner.

Policies are managed under **Organization → IAM → Policies**.
