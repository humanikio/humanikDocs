---
title: Roles
description: The built-in roles, what each is for, and how to vary one safely.
---

A role is a named bundle of policies. It says what is permitted **in principle**.
Where those permissions apply is decided by the
[assignment](/docs/access-control/assignments), not by the role.

Built-in roles ship with the platform and resolve before custom ones.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Built-in roles and policies cannot be modified or deleted.** This is what
  makes them safe to reason about: a role name means the same thing in every
  tenant. To vary one, fork it into a custom role.
</Callout>

## Built-in roles

Grouped by what they are for.

### Organisation administration

Assigned at `console`. Console Admin, Members Manager, IAM Manager and Console Viewer do not
descend into workspaces. Full Admin covers both.

The console shows each role by its name. The API, and any `roleId` you send, uses the id.

| Role | `roleId` | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Console Admin | `console-admin` | Full organisation level administration |
| Members Manager | `members-manager` | Inviting and managing people, without wider console rights |
| IAM Manager | `iam-manager` | Defining roles and policies, and making assignments |
| Console Viewer | `console-viewer` | Read only access to members, access control and settings |
| Full Admin | `full-admin` | Everything Console Admin and Workspace Admin can do, together |

### Workspace and membership

| Role | `roleId` | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workspace Admin | `workspace-admin` | Full control of one workspace and everything inside it |
| Viewer | `viewer` | Read only. It sees which credentials exist but never their values, and it does not see an office's machine or its logs |

### Operational

Narrower roles for a specific job, meant to be assigned at a narrow boundary.

| Role | `roleId` | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office Operator | `office-operator` | Running and managing offices: prompting them, starting and stopping them, changing their setup, their machine and its logs, and their credentials including the values |
| Workflow Operator | `workflow-operator` | Running workflows |
| Data Source Manager | `datasource-manager` | Managing data sources and ingestion |
| Integration Publisher | `integration-publisher` | Publishing plugins for others to use |

A role that lets someone use Nova does not widen what Nova may do for them. Nova
can do only what the role's other permissions allow. For example,
Office Operator includes `nova:manage` but no data source permissions, so a
person who holds only that role can ask Nova to read a namespace's data and Nova
will be refused. See [When Nova acts for you](/docs/access-control/acting-for-you).

### Machine identities

Assigned to service accounts rather than to people. Usually attached
automatically when the office or browser is created.

| Role | `roleId` | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office Agent | `office-agent` | The identity an AI employee's office runs as |
| Browser Agent | `browser-agent` | The identity a cloud browser runs as |
| Third-Party Agent | `third-party-agent` | The default for a [connected agent](/docs/connected-agents), such as Muse or Grok Bot |

Office Agent covers what an AI employee does on your behalf: group chats,
contacts and opportunities, calendars, outbound messages and text messages from
its own assigned addresses and numbers, **placing and ending phone calls**, the
databases connected to its office, and the app containers it builds and runs,
including their environment variables. It is scoped to a single workspace.

It can change and delete records, not only read them. That covers contacts, and
the records and tables in databases connected to its office.

Phone calls are worth calling out, because it is the permission with the largest
reach outside your workspace. An employee can place a call only from a number
assigned to its office, and only while that office has one. If you do not want an
employee dialling out, do not assign it a number.

It stops short of administrative actions. It cannot
delete a project or container, manage workspace settings, add or remove chat
members, or send from an address it was not assigned. Those stay with people.

Browser Agent is a good illustration of the model working. It is scoped to
inference and the databases allocated to that browser, and it explicitly excludes
chat, contacts, calendars, and office access. A browser that is compromised
cannot reach the rest of the workspace, because its role never permitted it.

Third-Party Agent is kept small, because a connected agent runs on
a computer Humanik does not control. It can read its workspace, message the
workspace's AI employees in HumaChat, and post in channels it is added to. It cannot
change records, send email or text messages, read credentials, or manage access. The
person approving the agent can pick a different role instead.

### Credentials: names and values are separate

Seeing that a credential exists and reading what it contains are two different
permissions.

| Action | Lets you |
| --- | --- |
| `secrets:list` | See each credential's name and the names of its fields |
| `secrets:read` | Reveal a value. It also allows everything `secrets:list` does |
| `secrets:write` | Add, change and remove credentials |

Viewer holds `secrets:list` only. In the role editor, **View** on the Credentials
row grants `secrets:list`, and **Reveal values** is its own checkbox, so revealing is
never included by accident.

<Callout type="warn">
  **A custom role you made with `secrets:read` can still reveal values.** If it was
  meant to be read only, switch it to `secrets:list`.
</Callout>

**A `roleId` that names no role grants nothing, and is not rejected.** Creating an
assignment or a service account with a misspelled id, such as `officeOperator` for
`office-operator`, succeeds. The identity then gets `403` on everything that role was
meant to allow. Copy ids from the tables above, or from your custom roles list.

## Choosing one

Two questions, in this order:

1. **What is the narrowest role that covers the job?** Start from the operational
   roles. Reach for Workspace Admin (`workspace-admin`) only when the job really is "everything in
   this workspace".
2. **What is the narrowest boundary it can be assigned at?** This matters more
   than the choice of role. `viewer` at `workspace/W1` is far wider than
   `office-operator` at one office.

A common mistake is picking a narrow role and then assigning it at the workspace
root, which quietly grants it everywhere inside.

## Admin-level roles

A role is admin-level when it can manage access control (`iam:manage`). Whoever
holds one can create any role or policy and assign any role to anyone, including
themselves. In practice, holding an admin-level role is close to owning the account.

The built-in admin-level roles are Console Admin, IAM Manager and Full Admin. A
custom role becomes admin-level as soon as one of its policies grants managing
access control. The IAM pages mark these roles with an **Admin-level** badge.

The platform decides which roles are admin-level, in one place. The IAM pages, the
role summary, Nova and [notifications](/docs/notifications) all read that same
answer, so they always agree.

Admin-level roles also decide who receives operational alerts, such as a calendar
account that stopped syncing. They go to admins whose admin role reaches the
resource in question, so an admin of one workspace is not alerted about another.

The permission system treats them like any other role: anyone who can manage access
control can assign one on the IAM pages. The control is **who holds them**, so keep
that list short.

<Callout type="warn">
  **Only a person can give out an admin-level role.** Nova can prepare other access
  changes for you to approve, but it will not grant, create or assign anything that
  can manage access control. It can remove an admin-level role, because that lowers
  access. See [Changing access with Nova](/docs/nova/changing-access).
</Callout>

## Forking a built-in

When a built-in is close but not right, copy it into a custom role rather than
trying to work around it.

In the console, go to **Organization → IAM → Roles** and create a new role. Choose
the built-in you want under **Start from**: its policies are copied into the new
role, and you add or remove from there. The page shows what the role can do, in
plain words, as you change it. Custom roles live in your tenant and resolve after
built-ins.

<Callout>
  Prefer forking over stacking. Assigning two roles to cover a gap works, but the
  result is harder to audit later, because no single object states what the
  identity may do.
</Callout>

## Policies inside a role

A policy is a list of statements. Each statement has an effect, a set of actions,
and a set of resources:

```json
{
  "effect": "allow",
  "actions": ["office:read", "office:manage"],
  "resources": ["workspace/W1/office/*"]
}
```

Deny statements are evaluated first and win outright. Use them to carve an
exception out of a broad allow, and remember that they do not constrain the
tenant owner.

Policies are managed under **Organization → IAM → Policies**. The editor has two
views of the same policy:

- **Full (allow and deny)** shows the statements themselves: Allow or Deny on each,
  its actions, and the places it applies to. A new policy opens here.
- **Simple (allow only)** shows each area, such as Contacts or Billing, at None, View,
  Use or Manage, with one set of places for the whole policy, and writes the statements
  for you. A policy that uses Deny, or applies different statements in different places,
  cannot be shown this way and opens in Full.

The places you can choose are listed under each workspace: its offices, databases,
browsers, calendars, channels and projects, or the whole workspace.

To change a custom policy, select **Edit** on its row in the list, or **Edit policy**
under its statements when the row is open. Built-in policies cannot be changed, so
they show **Open**. A change applies to every role that uses the policy, and the list
says how many do.

From a role's page you can work on its policies without leaving it:

- **The pencil on a policy** opens that policy in the editor.
- **New policy** creates one, then brings you back to the role with the new policy
  ticked. Save the role to add it.

If the role has changes you have not saved, both open in a new tab, so the changes
stay where they are.
