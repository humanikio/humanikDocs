---
title: When Nova acts for you
description: Nova has no permissions of its own. Every action it takes for you is checked against your permissions, by the same rules as an API call.
---

Nova does work for you inside HumanikOS: it reads a table, edits a document,
wakes an office, builds a workflow. It does that work as you. It holds no
identity, no roles and no credentials of its own.

Every action Nova takes that reads or changes something in your account is
checked against **your** permissions, using the same rules as a request to the
API. If you could not do it through the API, Nova cannot do it for you.

## Nova and AI employees are checked differently

Your own code and your AI employees call the API directly, each with its own
key, so every request they make has always been checked against that key's
permissions.

The difference is whose permissions count:

| | Acts with |
| --- | --- |
| **Nova** | Your permissions. It has none of its own |
| **An AI employee** | Its own permissions: the namespaces, integrations and access it was given. Not the permissions of the person who asked it for something |
| **Your code** | The permissions of the service account behind its key |

So asking an employee to do something you could not do yourself is refused or
allowed by what the employee was given, not by what you hold. Asking Nova is
refused or allowed by what you hold.

## Two permissions, not one

Using Nova and what Nova may do are separate.

| Question | Answered by |
| --- | --- |
| May you use Nova in this workspace at all? | `nova:manage` on the workspace. Without it, Nova does not run for you there |
| May Nova do this particular thing for you? | The permission the matching API endpoint needs, on the item it acts on |

Holding `nova:manage` does not add to what you can do. A person whose role
lets them use Nova but not edit a namespace's files can ask Nova to edit one,
and Nova will be refused, exactly as the API would refuse them.

The permission each action needs is the one listed for the matching endpoint
in the [API reference](/docs/api-reference). Editing a text file through Nova
needs `datasource:manage` on the namespace, the same as
[Edit a text file](/docs/api-reference/buckets/editing/editTextObject).
Waking an office needs what waking it through the API needs.

## The workspace is checked, not assumed

When Nova acts on a namespace, a browser, a calendar or a project, the platform
looks up which workspace that item really belongs to, and checks your
permissions there. Asking Nova in one workspace about a namespace that belongs
to another workspace is checked against the other workspace, and is refused if
you have no access to it.

## What you see when Nova is refused

Nova tells you it could not do the action and names the permission that was
missing, for example `datasource:manage`. Nothing was changed. To allow it,
someone who manages access control gives you a role that includes that
permission on the right workspace. See [Assignments](/docs/access-control/assignments).

## When nobody is watching

A [scheduled task](/docs/nova/scheduled-tasks) or a
[text message to Nova](/docs/nova/texting) has no signed-in session. The
platform rebuilds your permissions first, as described in
[Running as a user](/docs/access-control/running-as-a-user), and then every
action is checked the same way as above. Your permissions are read when the
work runs, so a task created before your access was narrowed is held to your
access as it is now.

Actions that wait for your approval are checked again when you approve, with
your permissions at that moment. See
[What Nova asks you to approve](/docs/nova/approvals).

## The sharp edges

**The account owner passes every check.** Owners are not subject to role
checks anywhere on the platform. To see what Nova will refuse for someone, test
with that person's account or another member account, never the owner's.

**Some checks are in addition to permissions.** A browser or a calendar is
shared with specific people and employees. Nova needs both the permission and
that membership, the same as the API.

**Nova still sees every capability.** It is not shown a reduced list based on
your permissions. It finds out an action is not allowed when it tries, and
tells you.
