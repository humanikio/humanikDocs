Document signing turns a PDF into something people can sign online. You prepare the
document once as a **template**, then send it as a **request** to the people who need to
sign. Each person gets an email with their own private link. They do not need an account.

When the last person has signed, everyone receives the finished document.

```
   Template                         Request
     PDF + boxes for each person  →   sent to real people
                                       ├─ Client     ana@example.com      signed
                                       └─ Manager    sam@acme.com         waiting
                                                    ↓
                                       Finished document + audit pages
```

## Templates

A template is one PDF, the people who sign it, and the boxes each of them fills in.

1. Open **HumaDocs** in a workspace, go to **Templates** and select **Create template**. Upload a
   PDF. It can be up to 25 MB and 200 pages.
   A password protected PDF cannot be used. Remove the password and upload it again.
2. Name the people who sign. These are **roles**, such as "Client" or "Manager", not real
   people yet. A template has between 1 and 10 roles.
3. Drag boxes onto the pages and give each box to a role.
4. Select **Save**.

There are seven kinds of box:

| Box | What the signer does |
| --- | --- |
| Signature | Draws, types or uploads a signature |
| Initials | Draws, types or uploads initials |
| Signed date | Nothing. The date is filled in when they finish, in their own time zone |
| Text | Types a short answer |
| Name | Types their name |
| Email | Types an email address |
| Checkbox | Ticks or leaves it empty |

A box can be required or optional.

The PDF of a template never changes. To use a different PDF, make a new template.
Archiving a template hides it from the list. Requests already sent from it are not
affected.

## Sending

Select **New document**, or **Use** beside a template. Choose a person for each role, with a
name and an email address. Select **Send**, or tick **Save as a draft** to send it later from
**Documents**.

**Order.** Each role has an order number. People with the same number sign at the same
time. People with a higher number are emailed only after everyone before them has
finished. If every role has the same number, everyone is emailed at once.

**Expiry.** You can set a date after which the request can no longer be signed. It must
be between one hour and one year ahead.

**Reminders.** People who have not signed yet are reminded every 2, 3 or 7 days, or not
at all. The default is every 3 days. Each person gets at most 3 reminders, and none after
the request expires.

A sent request keeps its own copy of the template. Changing the template later does not
change what people were sent.

## What signers see

The link opens the document in the browser. The signer:

1. Agrees to sign electronically. Nothing can be filled in before that.
2. Fills in their own boxes. They cannot see or change boxes that belong to someone else.
3. Selects **Finish**. Required boxes must be filled in first.

A signer can also **decline**, with a reason. Declining stops the whole request, and the
person who sent it is told by email.

<Callout type="info">
  **Each reminder has a new link, and the old link stops working.** A signer who opens an
  older email sees that the link no longer works. They should use the newest email.
</Callout>

## The finished document

When everyone has signed, Humanik OS makes the finished document: the original PDF with
every answer and signature drawn onto it, followed by **audit pages**.

The audit pages list:

- the request name, and when it was created and finished
- the original file, its page count and its SHA-256 hash
- for each signer: name, email, role, when they signed (in UTC and their own time zone),
  their IP address and browser, and when they agreed to sign electronically
- every step of the request, in order

The finished document's own SHA-256 hash cannot be printed inside the document, because
printing it would change it. It is shown on the request page and in the email.

**Delivery.** Each signer receives an email with their own download link, which works for
30 days. The document is never sent as an attachment. Members with access can download it
from the request page at any time.

**The seal.** The finished document carries a digital signature from Humanik OS. Any PDF
reader can use it to tell whether the file was changed after it was finished. Adobe Reader
shows the signer's identity as unknown, because the certificate is not on Adobe's trusted
list. It still reports whether the document was changed.

Signatures, answers and the finished document are stored encrypted.

## Checking a copy

Anyone who receives a copy of a finished document can check that Humanik OS made that
exact file. Open the verify page at `/sign/verify` in the Humanik OS app (a finished
request links to it as **Check a copy**) and choose the file. No account is needed.

The file is not uploaded. The browser computes its SHA-256 hash and only the hash is
checked. The answer says whether the file is known, the document name, who sent it and
when it was finished. It never shows the signers' details.

A file that was changed in any way, even by one byte, is not recognised.

Deleting a request removes the documents, signatures and answers. A copy of its finished
document can still be checked afterwards.

## Who can do what

Three actions control document signing in a workspace:

| Action | Lets someone |
| --- | --- |
| `signing:read` | See templates and requests, who has signed, and download finished documents |
| `signing:send` | Send a template, send a request to a signer again, and cancel a request |
| `signing:manage` | Create, change and archive templates, and delete requests |

Signers need none of these. Their link is their access. See
[Actions](/docs/access-control/actions) for how these are granted.

## Limits

- Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters are not drawn onto the finished document yet.
  They appear as question marks, and the audit pages say so. The answers themselves are
  kept as typed.
- The time inside the seal comes from the Humanik OS server. No outside timestamp service
  is used.
- A request cannot be cancelled once everyone has signed.
