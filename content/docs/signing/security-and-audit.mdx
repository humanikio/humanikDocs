A signed document is only useful if three things hold: nobody but the right people could see
or sign it, there is a record of who signed and when, and nobody can change it afterwards
without that showing. This page explains how HumaDocs does each.

<SigningLifecycle />

## Who can open a document

**People in your workspace** reach templates and documents only through
[permissions](/docs/signing#who-can-do-what). Every request is checked against the
workspace the document belongs to.

**Signers** reach a document only through their own link. A link is a long random value,
192 bits, so it cannot be guessed. It opens one document, for one person, and shows only that
person's fields.

- HumanikOS stores a SHA-256 fingerprint of each link to look it up. A fingerprint cannot be
  turned back into the link.
- The current link is also kept encrypted, so a member with `signing:send` can copy it from
  the document page without breaking it.
- Making a new link (**Resend**, **New link**) stops the previous one at once. A reminder sends
  the same link.
- A link stops working when its person finishes or declines, and when the document is
  cancelled or expires.
- In a signing order, every person has a link from the start, but a link can be used to sign only
  when it is that person's turn. Before that, the page shows only who signs first.
- The signing pages limit how often each address and each link can be used.

The signing pages are not indexed by search engines, and they do not send the link to any
other site the page loads from.

## Encryption

Everything a signer enters is encrypted the moment it is saved:

| Encrypted | When |
| --- | --- |
| Every value a signer types or ticks | On each save |
| Every signature and initials image | When it is adopted or placed |
| Values the sender filled in before sending | When the draft is saved |
| Each person's current link | When the link is made |
| The finished document | When it is made |

Encryption uses AES-256-GCM with a key kept apart from every other HumanikOS secret. Each
encrypted value is bound to the document and the person it belongs to: copied anywhere else,
it cannot be read, so one person's signature can never appear as someone else's. Anything
that fails this check is refused, never shown and never drawn onto a document.

The PDF you upload, and each document's copy of it, are kept in private storage. They are
not encrypted with the signing key, because they hold your own content and no signer's data.

## The audit pages

Every finished document ends with audit pages, added after the last page of your PDF. They
record:

- the document name and its ID, the workspace, when it was created and when everyone signed
- the original file: its name, page count and SHA-256 fingerprint
- for each signer: name, email address and role, when they signed (in UTC and in their own
  time zone), the internet address and browser they signed from, when they agreed to sign
  electronically, and the exact words they agreed to
- every event, in order: sent, opened, agreed, signed, reminded, declined, and how each link
  was delivered

The same record is on the document page in HumaDocs and through the
[API](/docs/api-reference/signing/activity/listSigningEvents). Entries are added as things
happen and are not edited afterwards.

The finished document's own fingerprint cannot be printed inside it, because printing it
would change it. It is shown on the document page and in the email each signer receives.

## The digital seal

The finished document carries a digital signature from HumanikOS, the kind a PDF reader
shows in its signatures panel. We call it the seal.

<SealCheck />

**What it proves.** The file has not changed since HumanikOS made it. Change one byte, and
the reader reports that the document was altered. Nobody can make a new seal for a changed
file, because only HumanikOS holds the private key.

**Checking it needs nothing from us.** The public certificate travels inside the PDF, so a
reader checks the seal offline. See [Checking a finished document](/docs/signing/verify).

**What a reader says about the signer.** Adobe Reader reports whether the document changed.
It may also say the signer's identity is unknown, because our certificate is not on Adobe's
list of trusted certificates. That message is about who issued the certificate, not about
whether the document is intact.

**The time in the seal** comes from the HumanikOS server. No outside timestamp service is
used.

## SHA-256 fingerprints

A SHA-256 fingerprint is a 64 character value worked out from every byte of a file. The same
file always gives the same fingerprint, and changing even one byte gives a completely
different one. It cannot be turned back into the file.

HumaDocs records the fingerprint of the original PDF and of the finished document. The
finished document is checked against its fingerprint every time it is downloaded, and anyone
can check a copy against it on the verify page.

## Copies and deletion

Signers get the finished document through a download link that works for 30 days, never as
an email attachment, so the signed file does not sit unencrypted in every mailbox along the
way. Members with `signing:read` can download it from the document page at any time.

Deleting a document removes its files, its values, its signatures and its activity. A copy
of a finished document can still be checked on the verify page afterwards, which shows that
the sender has since deleted it.

## Legal standing

HumaDocs records what is commonly used to show that an electronic signature is valid: that
the signer agreed to sign electronically, the exact words they agreed to, that they acted to
sign each field, who they were sent to, when and from where they signed, and a seal that
shows the document has not changed since. Whether a signature is enforceable depends on
where you are and on the kind of document. Some documents must still be signed on paper.
Ask your own legal adviser if you are not sure.
