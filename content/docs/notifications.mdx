---
title: Notifications
description: The bell in the console, what puts something in it, and how access control decides who is told.
---

A **notification** is an item in the bell at the top of the console. It pings
when it arrives, shows a short summary, and takes you to the thing it is about
when you click it.

Notifications belong to a workspace. You see a workspace's notifications while
you are working in it, and each one is about something inside that workspace: a
chat, an office, a calendar.

<Callout>
  **Notifications are shown in the console only.** Nothing is sent by email or
  text message yet, so an alert reaches you the next time you open the console.
</Callout>

## What sends a notification

| You are told when | Who is told | Clicking it opens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Someone sends you a private message | The other person in the conversation | The conversation |
| A message arrives in a group chat you are in | Every person in the chat except the sender | The chat |
| An office's integration call fails | People who can read that office | The office's logs, filtered to that integration |
| A calendar account stops syncing | Admins who can manage that calendar | The calendar's Sources tab |

In a group chat, a message from an AI employee notifies the people in the chat
the same way a person's message does.

## Who is told is decided by access control

Chats have a member list, and being in a chat is what lets you read it, so chat
notifications go to the members.

Everything else has no member list. Telling the whole account would reach people
who cannot open the thing, and a hand-kept list goes out of date the moment
someone's role changes. So each of these notifications names an
[action](/docs/access-control/actions) on a resource, and the recipients are
exactly the people whose roles allow that action on that resource.

**If you could not open the page a notification links to, you are not sent it.**
The check is the same one that decides whether you can open that page, so the
two cannot disagree, and nobody has to maintain a list.

| Notification | Action | Resource | Audience |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Integration call failed | `office:read` | The office | Everyone who qualifies |
| Calendar account stopped syncing | `calendar:manage` | The calendar | Admins only |

### Everyone who qualifies, or admins only

A notification is sent to one of two audiences.

**Everyone who qualifies** is every person whose roles allow the action on the
resource. It suits information anyone entitled to the resource should have. An
integration failure goes to everyone who can read the office, because they can
read the logs it links to, even if fixing the integration needs more access than
they have.

**Admins only** narrows that to people holding an
[admin-level role](/docs/access-control/roles#admin-level-roles), and it is the
admin role itself that must allow the action on the resource. Someone who is an
admin of one workspace and an ordinary member of another is not treated as an
admin of the second. This audience suits operational alerts: something is broken
and a person who runs the account should look at it. A sync problem is not worth
interrupting every member who can read the calendar.

The account owner always qualifies. Service accounts are never sent
notifications, because nothing reads them. A single notification reaches at most
50 people.

## How often you are told

Each kind of notification is limited in its own way, so a busy chat or a failing
account does not bury everything else.

| Notification | How often |
| --- | --- |
| Private message | Once per message |
| Group chat | Once per chat until you read it. Later messages update the same notification and add to its count without pinging again. Reading the chat starts a new cycle |
| Integration call failed | Once per failed call. An integration that keeps failing keeps notifying |
| Calendar account stopped syncing | Once per outage, described below |

### Calendar sync alerts

A calendar's bound Google or Outlook account can stop syncing. New meetings on
that account then stop reaching the calendar, and meetings added directly in
Google or Outlook stop starting its automations.

| What went wrong | What happens | When admins are told |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The provider refused the connection, for example because access was revoked | The account is marked **Reconnect needed** and is not retried, because only binding it again can fix it | At once |
| Anything else, such as an outage at the provider | The account is marked **Retrying** and tried again after 30 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours, and then every 6 hours | After the third failure in a row, about three and a half hours in |

You are told once per outage. When the account syncs again it is back to normal,
and if it fails again later, that is a new outage and admins are told again. A
short outage that fixes itself on the next retry never sends anything.

See [Calendars and booking](/docs/calendars#when-a-connection-fails) for what to
do when you receive one.

## Reading and clearing

In the bell you can open a notification, which marks it read, remove one with its
cross, mark everything read, or clear everything. The unread count and the sound
follow what is still unread.

There are no per-type settings yet, so a notification cannot be muted.
