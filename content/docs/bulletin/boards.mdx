---
title: Boards
description: Filing tiles, arranging a page around them, and deciding what the workspace opens on.
---

A board is a named group of tiles, shown as a tab along the top of the
bulletin. A workspace can run on one board or on several: Operations, Content,
Reference.

Tiles are filed onto a board when they are posted, and can be moved between
boards later. A tile on no board is not lost: it sits on the un-grouped board
until somebody files it.

## Laying a board out

A board holds tiles. Its **page** is the arrangement around them: the headings,
the sections, the order, and which tile gets the width.

That page is written once and kept, rather than being reassembled every time
somebody looks. Laying a board out is therefore an action you take, not
something that happens continuously.

You lay a board out when the set of tiles on it changes:

- A tile was posted to it
- A tile was moved onto it or off it
- A tile was removed
- A tile was pinned, and should now lead the page

You do **not** lay a board out to refresh it. The contents are read live
whenever somebody opens the board, so a number on the page is current whether
the page was arranged this morning or last month.

<Callout>
  Lay a board out **once** after a run of changes rather than once per tile.
  Each pass costs a generation and rearranges the page under anybody reading
  it.
</Callout>

### The page never holds your data

The arrangement is written without ever being shown a row, a total, or a value
from any tile. It is given the titles and the shapes, and it writes headings
and empty places for the tiles to sit in.

Every place on the page is filled by the tile itself when somebody opens it.
This is why a page cannot go stale and cannot quote a number that has since
changed: it has never seen one.

If a tile is removed after a page was arranged, its place remains and says so.
The page stays readable, and laying the board out again closes the gap when you
are ready.

## Steering the arrangement

A board can be laid out with an instruction, which applies to the whole pass:

> Keep it dense, this is read on a phone.

This is weaker than a tile's own prompt. See
[Tile prompts](/docs/bulletin/prompts).

## Order decides what the workspace sees

Boards appear in an order you set, and **the first board is where the workspace
lands**. Reordering is not a personal preference: it changes what every person
in the workspace opens onto.

<Callout type="warn">
  Putting an empty board first means everybody opens the bulletin to an empty
  page. Check what is on a board before promoting it.
</Callout>

## Deleting a board keeps its tiles

Deleting a board does not delete what was on it. The tiles are un-grouped and
survive, and they need filing somewhere or they stay off every board people
navigate to.

This is deliberate. Tiles are addresses into your data, and a tidy-up should
never be the thing that quietly removes access to a document somebody relied
on. Removing a tile is a separate action, and even that removes only the tile:
the table, file, or bucket it pointed at is untouched.
