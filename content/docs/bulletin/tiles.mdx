---
title: Tiles
description: What a tile points at, and why it never holds a copy of your data.
---

A tile is an address and an intention. It records what to show and how the
person wants it read. It does not record the data.

That distinction is the whole design. A tile pointing at a table of orders has
no orders in it. When somebody opens the board, the table is read and the tile
draws what it finds.

## The four things a tile can point at

| Points at | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A table** | Rows in a [namespace](/docs/namespaces) | This week's job sites |
| **A file** | A document, spreadsheet, or image in a bucket | The signed proposal |
| **A saved query** | A question asked of a table, rather than the whole table | Orders over 5000, this month |
| **A note** | Text somebody wrote | The winter dispatch schedule |

The first three are live. The fourth is not, and that is deliberate: an
announcement should say what its author wrote, not change under them.

<Callout>
  A file is addressed by its **path**, not by an internal identifier. Replacing
  the file at that path is the ordinary way to update a document tile, and it
  keeps working when somebody renames or re-uploads.
</Callout>

## What the tile decides, and what it does not

A tile stores:

- **What it points at.** The table, path, query, or note text.
- **A prompt**, if somebody wrote one. How they want it read. See
  [Tile prompts](/docs/bulletin/prompts).
- **How it is currently drawn**, and a sentence saying why that was chosen.
- **Whether it is pinned**, and which board it is filed on.

It does not store rows, totals, titles taken from the data, or anything read
out of the source. Nothing in a tile can be out of date with the thing it
points at, because nothing in it was copied from there.

## How a tile gets its appearance

When a tile is posted, its data is read and a presentation is chosen from what
is actually there rather than from the source type alone. The available
presentations are:

`table` · `list` · `metric` · `chart` · `document` · `image` · `gallery` · `note`

A spreadsheet with one number in it is a metric, not a table. A bucket of
photographs is a gallery. A PDF is a document with a way to open it.

The choice is made once and stored, along with the reason. It is made again
when the tile changes: a new source, or a new prompt. It is **not** made again
when the underlying data changes, because the data changing is the normal case
and re-deciding the presentation every time would make the board move around
under whoever is reading it.

## Reading a tile is not the same as seeing it

A tile on a board is visible to anyone who can read the bulletin. Its
**contents** are not.

When somebody opens a board, each tile fetches its own data, and that fetch is
checked against the reader's own access to the namespace behind it. Somebody
without `datasource:read` on that namespace sees the tile and a short note in
place of the contents.

This means a board can be shared broadly without widening anybody's reach into
the data. See [Permissions](/docs/bulletin/permissions).
