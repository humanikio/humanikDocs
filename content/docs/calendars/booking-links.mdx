---
title: Booking links
description: A public page where someone outside your account picks a slot on your calendar.
---

A **booking link** is a page you share. Someone opens it, sees the times you are
actually free, and picks one. What they book becomes a real event on a real
account, and it can start an [automation](/docs/workflows).

Nothing about the page is a copy of your calendar. Availability is worked out
from the accounts bound to the calendar at the moment the visitor looks, so a
meeting you accepted a minute ago has already removed its slot.

## Where to find them

Booking links belong to a calendar, so they are managed on that calendar.

<Steps>

<Step>
**Open the calendar.** Assets, then Calendars, then the calendar you want.
</Step>

<Step>
**Go to Manage, then the Booking links tab.** This tab needs the manage
permission on the calendar.
</Step>

<Step>
**Create one with New.** You need at least one connected account bound under
Sources first. A booking link has to have somewhere to write the appointment,
and a calendar with nothing bound has nowhere.
</Step>

</Steps>

Anyone who can read the calendar also sees the links on the workspace calendar
page, where they can copy a link or take an embed snippet but not change
anything.

## Two kinds of link

Both are the same thing configured differently.

| | Reusable | One-time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who it is for | Anybody. Put it in a signature, on a site, in a campaign | One named person |
| How many bookings | As many as your caps allow | One, then it stops working |
| Its address | The name you choose | A long unguessable string |
| Where it comes from | You create it | You mint it from a reusable link |

A one-time link is a **snapshot**. It copies the settings as they stand the
moment you create it, so editing the original afterwards does not reach links you
have already sent. That is the point of it, and it is also the thing that
surprises people: fixing a typo on the reusable page does not fix the invitation
already sitting in someone's inbox.

<Callout type="warn">
  **A link's address must be unique across the whole platform**, not just your
  account. An address someone else has taken is refused even though it is not
  taken by you. Prefer something specific to something generic.
</Callout>

Changing the address of a link you have already shared breaks every copy of it
that is out there. The console keeps the field locked behind a deliberate
"Change URL" step for that reason.

## Deciding when people can book

Availability comes from five things, and knowing which one to reach for saves a
lot of guessing.

| To block out | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A recurring day, or part of one | Weekly hours. A day switched off has no availability at all |
| One specific date | A date exception, set to Closed |
| Time you are genuinely busy | Nothing. Real events on the bound accounts already remove those slots |
| Breathing room around each booking | A buffer before, after, or both |
| Volume | A cap per day, or per time slot |

### Weekly hours

Every day has its own switch and its own hours, so a shorter Friday needs no
workaround. Switching a day on copies the hours you are already using, and you
can change them from there.

A day can hold **more than one stretch of time**. Add a second one to keep a
lunch break bookable-around:

```
Mon   9:00am to 12:00pm     1:00pm to 5:00pm
Tue   9:00am to 5:00pm
Fri   9:00am to 1:00pm
Sat   off
```

Two stretches on the same day must not overlap. Overlapping ones would offer the
same start time twice on the booking page, so the console refuses to save them.

### Date exceptions

A date exception is a single date that ignores the weekly hours entirely. Set it
to **Closed** for a holiday, or give it its own hours for a day you are working
differently.

An exception **replaces** that day's weekly hours rather than adding to them,
which is what makes Closed mean closed even on a day you normally work.

The part you do not configure is the useful part: **anything already in your
calendar removes the slots it covers.** Blocking Thursday afternoon is a matter
of putting something in your own calendar.

An event marked free, rather than busy, does not remove anything. That is
deliberate, so a day held open with a placeholder still takes bookings.

<Callout type="warn">
  **A day with a cap cannot be rescheduled within itself.** A booking being moved
  still counts toward the day it is leaving, so once the cap is reached, moving
  it to a later slot the same day is refused. Moving it to another day works.
</Callout>

## What the booker sees afterwards

Under **After booking** you choose one of two things.

| | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Show a message** | The page confirms the booking and shows your text. The default reads "Your booking is confirmed." |
| **Send them to a page** | The browser goes to a URL you give, so you can use your own thank-you page and count the conversion there |

A redirect needs a full address starting with `http://` or `https://`.

<Callout>
  **A redirect behaves correctly inside an embed.** The page around the widget
  navigates, not just the widget, so the visitor does not end up looking at your
  thank-you page in a small box. This needs the embed script rather than a plain
  frame. See [Embedding a booking page](/docs/calendars/embedding).
</Callout>

Either way the booker also gets a link of their own for rescheduling and
cancelling.

## Rescheduling and cancelling

The link the booker keeps is the only handle they need, and it stays live after
the booking is made. They can move the appointment to another open slot, or
cancel it, without an account and without contacting you.

Their page reads the **real event**, not a stored copy of it. If you drag the
appointment to a different time in Google or Outlook, that is the time they see.

## Turning one off, and deleting it

**Turning a link off** stops it taking new bookings and leaves everything already
booked alone. This is usually what you want.

**Deleting a link** removes the link, its public address, and any one-time links
minted from it. Appointments already on the calendar are never touched: two
people agreed to that meeting, and tidying up a link is not a reason for it to
vanish.

<Callout>
  **Deleting a link that has bookings ahead of it is refused, and tells you how
  many.** That is a check, not a failure. You can delete it anyway, but the
  people who booked lose their reschedule and cancel link, so cancelling those
  bookings first is usually kinder.
</Callout>

## Doing this from the API

Every booking link operation is available programmatically, so you can create a
page per campaign, per territory, or per new hire without opening the console.

| To | Use |
| --- | --- |
| See what exists | [List booking links](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/listBookingLinks) |
| Make one | [Create a booking link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/createBookingLink) |
| Read one back | [Get a booking link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/getBookingLink) |
| Change its settings | [Update a booking link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/updateBookingLink) |
| Stop or restart it | [Enable or disable a booking link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/setBookingLinkEnabled) |
| Mint one for a named person | [Mint a single-use link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/generateSingleUseLink) |
| Remove it | [Delete a booking link](/docs/api-reference/calendars/booking-links/deleteBookingLink) |

Each of these takes the ID of the calendar the link belongs to. Get it from
[List calendars](/docs/api-reference/calendars/calendars/listCalendars).

The page a booker's browser calls is documented too, under the **Public booking**
tag. You only need it if you are building your own booking interface rather than
sharing or embedding ours.

| To | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Read a page's public settings | [Read a public booking page](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/getPublicBookingLink) |
| Get open times | [List open slots](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/listPublicBookingSlots) |
| Book one | [Book a slot](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/bookSlot) |
| Look up a booking | [Read a booking](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/getBookingByToken) |
| Move it | [Move a booking](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/rescheduleBooking) |
| Cancel it | [Cancel a booking](/docs/api-reference/calendars/public-booking/cancelBooking) |

<Callout type="warn">
  **Those six take no key at all.** The slug, or the booker's own manage token, is
  the access. Treat a reusable slug as public, because it is.
</Callout>
