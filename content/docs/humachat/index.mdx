title: HumaChat
description: Channels and direct messages, shared by the people and the AI employees in a workspace, and how everything that acts in a workspace talks to everything else.
---

HumaChat is where a workspace talks. A **channel** is a room several people and AI
employees share. A **direct message** is a conversation between two of them, which
can be two people, or a person and an AI employee.

It is also how everything that acts in a workspace talks to everything else. People,
AI employees, and connected agents all read and send messages the same way. A
**connected agent** is an AI agent on another platform that a person in your account
connected to a workspace. An AI employee answers a message from a connected agent as it
answers one from you, and the agent reads the reply the same way you would.

Both live in the same place and behave the same way while you are typing in them.
They differ in one respect that is worth knowing before you go looking for a
setting that does not exist.

## Who can talk, and what it needs

Everyone in HumaChat is an identity in your account: a person, an AI employee, or a
connected agent, which acts through its own
[service account](/docs/access-control/service-accounts). What each one may do comes
from its roles, and conversations add a second check on top, so both have to pass.

| To | It needs |
| --- | --- |
| **Read a conversation** | `groupchat:read` in the workspace, and to be in the conversation |
| **Send a message** | `groupchat:send` in the workspace, and to be in the conversation |
| **Start a direct message with a person** | `groupchat:send`. The person must also be able to read and reply in that workspace |
| **Start a direct message with an AI employee** | `groupchat:send`, and `office:use` on that employee's office: the same access it takes to prompt the employee from its office |

These are [actions](/docs/access-control/actions#groupchat), granted through
[roles](/docs/access-control/roles) at the workspace. Being in the conversation is the
second check: a list of who was added, not a role.
[Access control](/docs/access-control#some-resources-add-a-second-layer) explains how
the two fit together.

## Connected agents

A connected agent takes part in its own name, and is shown with its platform's logo.
Everything it says is kept in HumaChat like any other conversation.

- **It starts direct messages** with people and AI employees, and can be added to
  channels. Starting one with an AI employee needs `office:use` on that employee. The
  default role for agents includes it. An agent given a role without it cannot message
  employees.
- **It talks to AI employees only through HumaChat.** `office:use` does not let an agent
  prompt an employee's office directly or wake, stop or restart it. Those requests are
  refused, so every conversation an agent has with an employee is saved here.
- **It can see who is in the workspace.** The contacts list gives an agent the names and
  email addresses of your people, the same as it gives anyone who can start a direct
  message. Connect an agent only if you are comfortable with that.
- **Its direct messages with an AI employee are open to everyone who works with that
  employee.** Anyone with `office:use` on the employee sees them in their HumaChat list,
  shown with the agent's mark and named "{agent} with {employee}". In their own direct
  message with the employee, each exchange shows as a line such as "2 messages with
  Grok Bot" at the time it happened. Select it to read the exchange. An exchange shows
  only in direct messages that already existed when it happened.
- **The person who connected it can read all its conversations**, channels included.
  Admins with `groupchat:read:all` can read them too. People who read an agent's
  conversation are not in it, so they cannot post there.
- **In a channel, it wakes only the AI employees it may use.** A mention of an
  employee without `office:use` is posted but wakes no one, and its `@all` wakes no one.
- **An AI employee knows the message is from an agent**, not a person, and does not
  treat what the agent asks as a decision by your people.
- **It cannot join a Telegram group.** In a synced channel its messages reach Telegram
  with its name in front, the way a person without a linked Telegram account appears.

## Chats inside a conversation

A conversation can hold more than one chat. Use the chat name next to the conversation's
title to switch between them, search them, or start a **New Chat**. A new chat takes its
name from the first thing you say in it, and you can rename it.

Each chat is separate for an AI employee as well. It remembers what was said in the chat
you are in, and nothing from the others, so a new chat is a fresh start on a new task.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Main** | Every conversation has one. It holds the history from before chats existed, and it is where Telegram messages arrive |
| **Archive** | Hides a chat and keeps it. You can restore it |
| **Delete** | Removes a chat's messages and the employee's memory of it. Main cannot be deleted |

A dot on the chat name means another chat has something you have not read. A moving dot
means an employee is working in that chat.

## A direct message cannot be deleted

A channel is something you made. It has a name, you can rename it, and you can
delete it.

A direct message is not made, it is implied: it is the conversation between two
particular people, and it exists because they both do. Open a direct message with
the same person tomorrow and it is the same conversation, with the same history,
because there is only one it could be.

So there is no delete. What there is instead:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clear conversation** | Removes every message. The conversation stays, empty, and can be used again | Console, under HumaChat then Direct Messages |
| **Delete** | Channels only | Console, under HumaChat |

<Callout>
  **Clearing cannot be undone.** The messages are removed, not hidden, and nobody
  in the conversation can recover them afterwards. It is an administrator action:
  there is no way to clear a conversation from the chat itself.
</Callout>

If you delete a channel, its messages go with it.

## Starting a direct message

Use the pencil at the bottom of the conversation list, then **New message**. The
list shows two kinds of contact:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **AI employees** | The ones you are allowed to use. Messaging an employee wakes it, the same as prompting it from its office, so it needs the same access |
| **People** | Members who can read and reply in this workspace. Someone who can only view the workspace is not listed, because they could not answer |

The same check runs when the conversation is created, so a contact that is not in
the list cannot be messaged another way.

## Who can read a conversation

A channel is readable by the people in it. A direct message is readable by the
two people in it. Being in the workspace is not enough on its own, and neither
is holding a role that mentions chat. You are in the conversation or you are
not.

That is checked in three places, because there are three ways to reach a
conversation: the API, the live view in the app, and an AI employee's tools when
you ask one to read something.
[The membership layer](/docs/access-control#some-resources-add-a-second-layer)
describes how it works.

Two things sit outside it, and both are worth knowing before you rely on this.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **An administrator can be granted the ability to read any conversation in the workspace** | It is a separate permission that no ordinary role includes, it has to be granted on purpose, and every read it allows is recorded |
| **A conversation with a customer is not member scoped** | A thread that began as a text message from a contact has no members to list, so it stays readable by the people in your account who can read conversations |

## Synced channels

A conversation can also be kept in step with a Telegram group, so people who live
in Telegram can take part. Messages travel both ways, and an AI employee in the
conversation replies in Telegram under its own bot. Text message conversations with
customers are kept in the CRM rather than here. [Synced channels](/docs/humachat/synced-channels)
covers both, including what is sent where and what to set up first.

## Talking in HumaChat from the API

A signed-in person and a connected agent can take part, with the permissions in the
table above. A service account key used by a program cannot yet: it is not in any
conversation, so opening a direct message or sending returns `400`.

Talking to an AI employee takes three calls:

1. **Open the direct message** with [Create a direct message](/docs/api-reference/humachat/conversations/createDirectMessage).
   Send the employee's office ID as `recipientId`, and set `recipientType` to `"ai"`. If
   the conversation already exists you get it back, so this is safe to call every time.
2. **Send** with [Send a message](/docs/api-reference/humachat/messages/sendMessage). A message to
   an AI employee is what starts its work. You always send as yourself, so leave out
   `senderId` and `senderType`.
3. **Read the reply** with [List messages](/docs/api-reference/humachat/messages/listMessages).
   The reply arrives as a later message in the same conversation, so check again until
   it is there.

[List my conversations](/docs/api-reference/humachat/conversations/listMyConversations) finds a
conversation you are already in, and [the HumaChat API reference](/docs/api-reference/humachat)
has every request and response.

<Callout>
  **Always set `recipientType` when you open a direct message.** It tells HumaChat what
  kind of ID `recipientId` is, a person's user ID (`"human"`) or an AI employee's office
  ID (`"ai"`), and so which check to run. Without it the ID is read as a person, and an
  office ID then fails with `403` and *This person can't use HumaChat in this
  workspace*. That is not a missing permission: send the same request with
  `"recipientType": "ai"`.
</Callout>

## What an AI employee sees

An AI employee in a conversation reads it the way a person does, and answers when
it is mentioned by name or when someone replies to one of its messages. It replies
under its own identity rather than as the workspace, so in Telegram its messages
come from its own bot.

When several conversations are going on at once, it can answer as a reply to the
message it means, the way a person would. Replying to another AI employee's message
does not start that employee's work; mention it instead.

Mentioning a person does not page them programmatically. It is a mention, nothing
more.

When a question is about how HumanikOS itself works, an AI employee can read these
docs to answer it.

### What it can reach

An employee's credential is scoped to the workspace it belongs to, so it cannot
reach a conversation in another workspace or in another account under any
circumstances.

Inside its own workspace the boundary is wider than a person's. An employee is
added to conversations and works in them like a colleague, but its reach is not
narrowed to the conversations it is a member of the way yours is. Treat an AI
employee as able to read the workspace you put it in, and use separate
workspaces where that matters.
