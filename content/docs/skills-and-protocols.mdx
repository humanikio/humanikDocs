---
title: Skills and protocols
description: What an employee can do, and what it must always do.
---

Two things shape how an AI employee behaves, and they are not the same shape.

> **A skill is a capability the employee reaches for. A protocol is a rule that
> applies whether it reaches for anything or not.**

Getting this the wrong way round is the most common modelling mistake, so it is
worth an example before the mechanics.

## The difference, concretely

Say you want an employee that handles refunds.

**"How to issue a refund in Stripe"** is a skill. It is a procedure, used when a
refund is the task at hand, and irrelevant the rest of the time.

**"Never refund more than $500 without human approval"** is a protocol. It is
true during a refund, during a report, and during a conversation that has nothing
to do with money. The employee does not choose to consult it.

Written as a skill, that rule only applies when the employee happens to load the
refund skill. Written as a protocol and attached to the work that issues refunds,
it is put in front of every one of those turns. The second is what you meant.
Exactly when a protocol is in force is under [Protocols](#protocols).

## Skills

### They are Agent Skills

A skill on HumanikOS is a directory containing a `SKILL.md` file: YAML
frontmatter naming and describing it, then a markdown body of instructions. That
is the [Agent Skills](https://agentskills.io/) layout, an open specification also
implemented by Microsoft's Agent Framework and others.

It matters for a practical reason. A skill you write is not trapped here. The
same shape is portable to any Agent Skills compatible product, and skills written
elsewhere are readable to you.

<Callout>
  **Further reading.** The
  [Agent Skills specification](https://agentskills.io/specification) defines the
  format itself. Microsoft's
  [Agent Skills guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agent-framework/agents/skills)
  is a good second perspective on the same idea, written against a different
  runtime.
</Callout>

**What we share with the specification:** one directory per skill, a `SKILL.md`
carrying frontmatter and a body, `name` and `description` as the identifying
fields, and the advertise-then-load pattern below.

**What is ours:** the fields around it. `contentSource` and its locking rules,
`requiresEnv` and `requiresBins`, the `userInvocable` and `modelInvocable` split,
and the office scoping are HumanikOS concepts rather than specification ones.
Our `description` is capped at 100 characters, which is tighter than the
specification allows.

We do not claim full specification compliance. We share its shape, and we say so
because knowing that shape makes the rest of this page make sense.

### How a skill reaches the employee

Skills are not all loaded into every conversation. That would spend the context
window on instructions the employee does not need.

Instead the employee sees the **name and description** of every skill it has, and
reads the **body** of one only when a task matches it. This is why the
description field earns its 100 characters: it is the only thing the employee has
to decide with.

How skills, plugins and the framework fit together on the office machine is on
[Inside an office](/docs/cloud/inside-an-office).

Write a description that says what the skill does **and when to use it**.
"Reconcile orders" is a label. "Match settled payments against open orders, for
nightly reconciliation or a payment dispute" is a decision.

### The fields

A skill is a named body of instructions attached to an office. It carries what it
needs to run: environment variables, binaries, and whether a person or the model
may invoke it.

Skills come from three places, recorded on each one as `source`:

| `source` | Means |
| --- | --- |
| `integration` | Generated from a plugin you connected |
| `custom` | You wrote it |
| `system` | The platform provides it |
| `protocol` | Made from one of your [protocols](#protocols) |

### A skill is instructions, not a connection

A skill is a document. It carries no credential, no host, and no ability to call
anything on its own.

What an employee can actually reach comes from an **plugin**: a host, an
authentication scheme, and a reference to a credential in a vault. Connecting one
generates a skill from it, so the two arrive together and are easy to treat as
one thing.

| | Skill | Plugin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is | A document | A connection |
| Holds | Instructions | A host, an auth scheme, a credential reference |
| Changing it | Changes what the employee tries | Changes what the employee can reach |
| Read | By the employee, when a task matches | By the office, on every call |

Editing a skill to describe an endpoint does not make that endpoint reachable,
and deleting a skill does not revoke a credential.

Skills generated from a plugin have rules of their own: how the body is
written, what it may not contain, and what a lock or a verification stamp means.
Those live in [Plugin skills](/docs/plugins/skills).

### Skills are attached per office

A skill belongs to one office, and an office is one employee. Two offices can
hold the same skill, and changing it in one does not change it in the other.

The employee reads its skills when it boots, so a new skill, or an edit to an
existing one, takes effect on the next start.

<Callout type="warn">
  **This is the same restart rule that applies to protocols and secrets.** If you
  change a skill and behaviour does not change, the office has not restarted.
</Callout>

See [What is attached to an office](/docs/offices-and-employees#what-is-attached-to-an-office)
for everything else that hangs off an office and how each one gets there.

## Protocols

A protocol is a behavioural rule you write for an office. It has a priority, so
you control which rule wins when two disagree. When you save it, HumanikOS turns
your text into a skill for that office (its `source` is `protocol`), rewritten by
an AI model into a clear set of instructions.

### When a protocol is in force

| Where the protocol is | What the employee sees |
| --- | --- |
| Attached to an AI subscription, or to a **Prompt Office** step in a workflow | The protocol is put in front of that turn, before the work. Several are ordered by priority, lowest number first, and the employee is told to follow the earlier one when they conflict |
| Applied by the employee itself, when you ask it to or it decides to | In force for the rest of that session |
| Any other turn, such as an ordinary chat message | Listed with the employee's skills by name and description. The employee may load it or may not |

**Attach a protocol to the work it governs.** A rule that must hold whenever a
subscription or workflow hands work to the employee belongs on that
subscription or that step. That is the only place it is put in front of every
turn.

**Skill names on a protocol are guidance.** You can name the skills a protocol
is about. Those names are given to the AI model that writes the protocol's
instructions. They do not limit where the protocol applies.

### What is not guaranteed

- **A protocol does not bind ordinary chat turns.** In a conversation that no
  subscription or workflow started, the employee sees the protocol only as a skill
  it may choose to read.
- **The text in force is a rewrite of yours.** The employee reads the version the
  AI model wrote from your text, not your exact words. Read the generated skill
  after you save a protocol, and make your text plain enough that a rewrite
  cannot change its meaning.
- **A protocol is an instruction, not a permission.** It shapes what the employee
  tries. What the employee can reach is still decided by its role. See
  [What this does not cover](#what-this-does-not-cover).

<Callout type="warn">
  **Protocols load when the office boots.** Adding or changing one does not
  affect a running employee until it restarts. The list endpoint returns
  `gatewayRestartNeeded` so you can tell whether what you are reading is what is
  actually in force.
</Callout>

## Choosing between them

Ask one question: **would this still matter if the employee were doing something
unrelated?**

| Yes, it must hold whenever this work is handed over | Protocol, attached to that subscription or step |
| --- | --- |
| Only when performing a specific task | Skill |

Some examples that fall each way:

| Rule | Which | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Always sign off as the Support team" | Protocol | True in every reply the work produces |
| "How to look up an order by email" | Skill | Only during a lookup |
| "Never contact anyone marked do not contact" | Protocol | Must hold in every turn that can send a message |
| "How to reconcile the nightly payment file" | Skill | One job |
| "Escalate to a human if unsure" | Protocol | Applies to every decision |

## What this does not cover

Skills and protocols shape behaviour. They do not grant access. An employee with
a skill describing how to read a database still cannot read it unless the
namespace is allocated to its office and its role permits it.

Instructions and permissions are separate systems on purpose. A skill that
overstates what an employee may do produces a `403`, not a breach.
